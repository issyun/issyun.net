research

[top]

Exploring what emerging technologies can do for music creation and analysis.

Neural networks can do so much more than generate entire songs from prompts. The field of Music Information Retrieval (MIR), embracing recent advancements in ML tech, has shown new possibilities for music analysis and education, documentation and preservation, co-creation, and interactive music experiences. As a lifelong musician, I find this new wave of findings incredibly exciting.

My current research focuses on Automatic Music Transcription (AMT), pattern recognition in music audio, and modal translation between the many representations of music.

music

[top]

I've learnt many instruments throughout my youth, and I've been producing electronic music ever since I discovered FL studio in middle school (it's still my main DAW, and it's insane how much it's evolved since then). Music is such an integral part of my world, and it also guides my academic pursuits as an AI researcher.

In typical ADHD fashion, I play a ton of different instruments but mastered none of them. Started out with piano and drums in elementary school, learned guitar and bass in high school, and received private lessons for jazz piano in my early twenties. I'm also a huge music theory nerd and love to indulge in conversations about esoteric harmonies and metric modulations.

I make soundtracks for my indie game studio, base0. Our first game, HYNPYTOL, takes heavy inspiration from the GBC-era pixel art aesthetics, and I had a blast mixing period-correct chiptune sounds with modern synths. Our next game, BATHYSPHERE, is set to launch in late 2026 and will feature a 1940s setting and knowledge-based exploration elements; its soundtracks will incorporate more acoustic instruments and sample flips of old records.

I play electric bass in my fusion band Time & Place. I consider the bass as my main instrument nowadays; playing at the intersection of harmony and groove is an experience you can't get from any other instrument.
